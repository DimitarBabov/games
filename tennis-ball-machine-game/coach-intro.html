Why we made it

We bought a Proton ball machine for George. He has been training with you for almost a year, and he likes hitting, but feeding balls from a machine gets boring for a 10-year-old very fast.

So we turned it into a game. Every drill is a level, he earns stars for balls that land in a target, and every tenth level is a harder boss level that unlocks the next world. Each level is saved as a program in the Proton app (L001 to L100), so starting one takes a few seconds.

How the levels are built

Four rules, taken from junior development and motor-learning research (ITF Play & Stay progressions, the Challenge Point idea, practice variability).

One thing changes at a time

From one level to the next we change only speed, spin, where the ball lands, the order, or the target size.

60708090 km/h

Same ball → pattern → random

Repeating the same ball builds the stroke. Fixed patterns add footwork. Random order is harder, but it carries over to matches.

Pass at about 7 out of 10

Kids learn fastest at roughly 60–80% success. Easier than that teaches little; harder than that frustrates him.

The target shrinks

Whole court first, then past the service line, then crosscourt or down the line, then 3 × 3 m corner boxes marked with cones.

How a session runs

  1. 10 minutes of warm-up and 20 easy balls, no scoring.
  2. Replay one old level to beat his best score.
  3. Two to four new levels. A parent counts the balls that land in the target.
  4. One fun level of his choice, then we write the scores down.

About 150–250 balls in 45–60 minutes. Three tries per level per session at most; if he misses three times, he plays the level before it once and we move on. A boss level opens only after 15 stars in that world.
